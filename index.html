Biography & Academic Background

Bridging Computational Methods and Humanistic Inquiry

Catherine Wong is a Digital Humanities researcher whose work explores how computational methods can complement traditional humanities approaches to generate new perspectives on language, culture and historical change. Her research is driven by methodological questions concerning data, evidence, interpretation and explanation, with applications spanning historical linguistics, corpus stylistics, cultural analytics and research software.

01

Computational Stylistics

Applying computational stylistic methods to investigate how linguistic and stylistic choices construct narrative and meaning in literary and cultural texts.

02

Historical Corpora & NLP

Transforming historical texts into computationally analysable datasets through corpus development and NLP.

03

Research Software

Translating computational methods into intuitive research tools for humanities

04

Language, Culture & Identity

Examining language as a site of cultural expression, identity formation, and social change

Digital Humanities Tools

Computing Humanities with Criticality

DramaScope 1.0

DramaScope is a web-based research platform for investigating language patterns and language change in Early Modern English. Integrating lexical, semantic, and discursive analyses within a single interactive environment, it enables researchers to explore language from individual words to evolving conceptual structures. Version 1.0 is demonstrated using a Shakespeare corpus and is designed to support additional Early Modern corpora in future releases.

Visit →

Features include:

  • Lexical analysis (KWIC & collocates)
  • Expression & phraseology analysis
  • Concept modelling
  • Diachronic language change
  • Reproducible analytical workflows

Text Visualiser

A real-time computational text analysis workbench calculating Type-Token Ratios (lexical richness), archaic vocabulary density indices, and average syllable distributions.
Built with live HTML5 Canvas frequency charts for English prose, speech, and poetry.

Visit →

Features include:

  • Live Type-Token Ratio (TTR) lexical richness scoring
  • Early Modern orthographic archaism dictionary scanner
  • Interactive canvas bar charts with custom text support

Sherlock Holmes's Door

Sherlock Holmes's Door is an interactive computational narratology teaching suite exploring how language, narrative structure, and point of view create suspense in Sherlock Holmes. Drawing on corpus linguistics and quantitative discourse analysis, it enables users to investigate the narrative mechanisms underlying detective fiction through interactive visualisations and guided activities.

Visit →

Features include:

  • Corpus exploration
  • Narrative structure analysis
  • TCU alignment
  • POV & suspense dynamics
  • Interactive learning
