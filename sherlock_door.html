Corpus Feature Extraction

1. Most Frequent Words After 'the'

Across 10 short stories from The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes (85,979 tokens, 2,094 TCUs), the word the is the most frequent word in the dataset (occurring 4,657 times).

Examining lexical patterns following the reveals that 'the door' is the most frequent noun phrase (66 times), surpassing 'the matter' (65) and 'the room' (46).

Narratological Analysis

In detective fiction narratology, 'the door' operates as the physical and structural threshold of the mystery. As Michael Cook (2011) analogises, detective fiction functions as a 'locked room mystery' where the opening of the door introduces the client and initiates the investigation.

3. Point of View & Suspense Dynamics

Turn Construction Units: Holmes vs Watson

Sherlock Holmes (SH) Dr Watson (JW)
Story Progression (Turn Construction Unit Blocks)

Narrative Enclosure Theory

According to Tzvetan Todorov's structural approaches and Hoeken & van Vliet (2000), suspense is the product of 'postponing the story's outcome' through strategic POV switching.

Watson's Framing & Red Herrings

Watson dominates the exposition and rising action. His limited observations create red herrings that mislead the reader.

Holmes' Deductive Climax

Towards the climax, Holmes's POV takes over completely as he corrects Watson's mistakes and reveals the deductive chain of truth.
